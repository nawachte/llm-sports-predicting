

# **NFL Week 5 Touchdown Scorer Market Analysis**

## **Broncos @ Eagles**

### **1\. Foundational Analysis**

#### **Offensive & Defensive Schemes**

The Denver Broncos offense, under coordinator Joe Lombardi, operates from an Air Coryell base, a system historically known for its vertical passing attack.1 However, the 2025 iteration has established a strong run-first identity, ranking fifth in the NFL with 143.3 rushing yards per game.2 This ground-and-pound approach is often facilitated by the use of a six-man offensive line, signaling a clear intent to control the line of scrimmage.4 This commitment to the run sets up play-action opportunities for quarterback Bo Nix, who has managed 7 passing touchdowns against 4 interceptions through four games.1

They will face an elite Philadelphia Eagles defense coordinated by Vic Fangio. Fangio's scheme is characterized by its prevalent use of two-high safety looks, a structure designed specifically to limit explosive pass plays and force offenses to execute long, methodical drives.6 The unit was dominant in 2024, ranking near the top of the league in PFF's grades for run defense, pass rush, and coverage.6 Their proficiency against the pass is particularly notable, as they allowed the fewest yards per attempt in the league last season.8 In the current season, their red zone defense has been exceptionally stout, permitting a mere 20% completion rate and a 33.3 passer rating to opposing quarterbacks inside the 20-yard line.4

On the other side of the ball, the Eagles offense, now guided by Offensive Coordinator Kevin Patullo, continues its run-heavy philosophy.9 Philadelphia ran the ball more than any other team in 2024 and has maintained that identity, ranking second in both rushing attempts (131) and rushing touchdowns (7) through the first quarter of the season.6 This ground dominance comes at the expense of aerial production, with the passing game ranking a dismal 31st in yards per game.2 The offense is engineered to control the clock and physically impose its will in high-leverage situations.

This physical offense will test a formidable Denver defense coordinated by Vance Joseph. Operating from a 3-4 alignment, the Broncos' defense has been one of the league's best, ranking second in points allowed (16.8 per game) and first in sacks (15).1 The unit's calling card is its ferocious pass rush, led by edge rushers Nik Bonitto and Jonathon Cooper, which generated the league's second-highest pressure rate in 2024\.13 Their success extends to the red zone, where they rank first in the NFL, having allowed touchdowns on just three of 12 opponent possessions (25%).1

#### **Pace & Projected Game Script**

Denver operates at a slightly faster-than-average tempo, ranking seventh in the league with 28.8 seconds per play.15 Conversely, Philadelphia is one of the league's most methodical teams, consistently letting the play clock wind down below 10 seconds before the snap.16

With the Eagles installed as 3.5 to 4.5-point home favorites and a low game total of 43.5, a positive game script for Philadelphia is the most probable outcome.1 This scenario would allow the Eagles to lean into their run-heavy identity, using Saquon Barkley and Jalen Hurts to control the clock and protect their lead. This projects a negative game script for the Broncos, which traditionally forces a team to become more pass-heavy. However, a potential contradiction exists between conventional game script theory and this specific matchup. The Eagles' primary defensive strength is their pass defense, while their run defense, though solid, is statistically more forgiving, ranking 21st in rushing yards allowed per game.2 Given that Denver's offensive strength is its fifth-ranked rushing attack, the Broncos may choose to defy convention and continue to run the ball even if trailing, attacking the relative vulnerability of the Eagles' defense while protecting their quarterback in a hostile road environment. This strategic choice could suppress the game's overall play volume and concentrate Denver's touchdown opportunities on the ground.

### **2\. Key Touchdown Factors**

#### **Red Zone Usage Analysis**

Both offenses have been effective inside the 20-yard line, but their methods and key personnel differ significantly. The Broncos have converted 66.7% of their red zone trips into touchdowns, while the Eagles have been a perfect 11-for-11 (100%).1

#### **Key Positional Mismatch**

The critical confrontation in this game is the Eagles' vaunted offensive line against the Broncos' league-leading pass rush.12 Philadelphia's front features four Pro Bowl-caliber players, yet they have surprisingly ranked just 23rd in sack percentage allowed (8.2%) on the league's fewest pass attempts.12 This suggests a vulnerability that Denver's defense, which leads the NFL with 15 sacks, is uniquely equipped to exploit.

Should the Broncos' pass rush consistently win this battle, it would significantly disrupt the Eagles' passing game. This pressure would likely reduce the touchdown probability for wide receivers A.J. Brown and DeVonta Smith. However, it could paradoxically increase the chances of a Jalen Hurts rushing touchdown. Hurts is elite at evading pressure, and his ability to scramble and turn broken plays into touchdowns is a core component of his value. Therefore, a dominant performance by the Denver front seven may not eliminate Philadelphia's scoring opportunities but rather concentrate them onto the legs of their quarterback.

#### **Critical Injury Impact**

The Broncos enter this contest in excellent health. Reserve tight end Lucas Krull (foot) is the only player ruled out.24 Key contributors like wide receiver Marvin Mims Jr. (hip/ankle) and guard Quinn Meinerz (illness) practiced in full and are expected to play, providing Denver with its full complement of weapons.24

The Eagles are managing more significant injuries. Starting linebacker Nakobe Dean (knee) remains out, a notable absence, though Zack Baun has performed at an All-Pro level in his stead.6 The more pressing concerns are on offense, where All-Pro tackle Lane Johnson (shoulder) and tight end Dallas Goedert (knee) were limited during the week.26 Both are expected to play, but their effectiveness is crucial. Goedert's status, in particular, warrants close monitoring. After missing practice Wednesday, his participation was limited Thursday before a full session Friday.27 A knee ailment could potentially limit his snap count or explosiveness. As the team's most efficient red zone receiver, any limitation could force a redistribution of his high-value targets, potentially benefiting A.J. Brown and DeVonta Smith.

## **Titans @ Cardinals**

### **1\. Foundational Analysis**

#### **Offensive & Defensive Schemes**

The Tennessee Titans offense, coordinated by Nick Holz, utilizes a West Coast system that has been profoundly ineffective through four games.29 The unit ranks last in the league in points per game (12.8), 31st in total yards, and has managed only three offensive touchdowns all season (one rushing, two passing).29 A primary culprit is the offensive line, which has surrendered a league-high 17 sacks, putting immense pressure on rookie quarterback Cam Ward.30

They will face an Arizona Cardinals defense, led by coordinator Nick Rallis, that has been a surprising bright spot. This 3-4 unit ranks sixth in the NFL in points allowed (18.5 per game).33 After struggling mightily against the run in 2024, the Cardinals have improved significantly, ranking ninth in rushing yards allowed this season following key offseason additions along the defensive line.34 While their pass rush generates pressure at a below-average rate (25th), they have still managed to record 13 sacks.34

The Cardinals offense, under coordinator Drew Petzing, also employs a West Coast scheme but has produced below-average results, ranking 23rd in scoring.33 The offense revolves around the dual-threat capabilities of quarterback Kyler Murray, who has accounted for 20 red zone opportunities through his arm and legs.37 The running game has been a weakness, ranking 22nd in yards per game.38

This struggling offense has a favorable matchup against a porous Tennessee defense. Coordinated by Dennard Wilson, the Titans' 3-4 unit has been one of the league's worst, ranking 28th in points allowed (30.0 per game) and 28th against the run.29 They have been especially vulnerable on the ground near the goal line, having surrendered a league-high six rushing touchdowns to opposing running backs through four weeks.39

#### **Pace & Projected Game Script**

Both teams operate at a deliberate tempo. The Titans rank 10th in seconds per play (29.0), while the Cardinals play even slower, ranking 22nd in plays per game (59.3).15

The market has established the Cardinals as heavy 7.5 to 8.5-point home favorites, with a low game total set between 41.5 and 42.5 points.35 This betting line implies a strong positive game script for Arizona and a deeply negative one for Tennessee. The Cardinals should be able to control the game on the ground against the Titans' weak run defense. Consequently, Tennessee will likely be forced into a pass-heavy approach with a struggling rookie quarterback behind a compromised offensive line. While this scenario points to continued inefficiency for the Titans, the sheer volume of passing attempts in a trailing script could create a "garbage time" touchdown opportunity against a Cardinals pass defense that ranks 28th in passing yards allowed.35

## **Vikings @ Browns**

### **1\. Foundational Analysis**

#### **Offensive & Defensive Schemes**

The Minnesota Vikings offense is built around a dynamic downfield passing game, but its effectiveness has been severely hampered by injuries along the offensive line.51 The unit has allowed a league-worst 14% sack rate, forcing quarterbacks into constant duress.51 With rookie J.J. McCarthy sidelined, veteran Carson Wentz has been under center, spreading the ball around effectively when given time but struggling with the consistent pressure.51 The offense has been particularly poor on third down, converting at the fourth-worst rate in the league (30.6%).51

They face a Cleveland Browns defense that has been utterly dominant under coordinator Jim Schwartz. Operating from a 4-3 base, Schwartz's "attack-style" scheme has propelled the Browns to the top of the league in several defensive categories.52 They rank first in total yards allowed (222.5 per game), first in rushing yards allowed (70.3 per game), and fourth in passing yards allowed (152.3 per game).54 The defensive line, led by All-Pro Myles Garrett and breakout star Maliek Collins, has been the engine, ranking first in PFF pass-rushing grade.52 Schwartz achieves this pressure with one of the league's lowest blitz rates (18.4%), relying on his front four to win their matchups while playing heavy Cover 1 behind them.55

The Cleveland Browns offense has been the polar opposite of its defense. Under coordinator Tommy Rees, the West Coast scheme has been anemic, ranking 31st in points per game (14.0) and 30th in rushing yards per game (84.8).53 After weeks of ineffective play from veteran Joe Flacco, the team is turning to rookie third-round pick Dillon Gabriel for his first career start.57

Gabriel will make his debut against a formidable Minnesota defense coordinated by Brian Flores. Flores is renowned for his aggressive, blitz-heavy schemes that are designed to create havoc and turnovers.58 The Vikings' defense ranks fifth in scoring defense and has been particularly effective at forcing turnovers, with a league-high five fumbles forced.58 While they have been susceptible to the run at times (24th in rushing yards allowed), their overall defensive structure is ranked first in schedule-adjusted efficiency.60

#### **Pace & Projected Game Script**

Both teams play at a slower-than-average pace, with the Vikings ranking among the six slowest teams in adjusted pace.16 The Browns are not much faster, ranking 5th in seconds per play.15

This game, being played in London, has the Vikings favored by 3.5 to 4.5 points with a very low total of 35.5-36.5 points.56 This implies a low-scoring, defensive struggle. The game script projects to be neutral to slightly positive for Minnesota. Given the strength of both defenses and the struggles of both offenses—particularly Cleveland's with a rookie QB making his first start—neither team is likely to build a large lead. This projects a conservative, run-heavy game plan from both sides, with a focus on field position and avoiding turnovers.
