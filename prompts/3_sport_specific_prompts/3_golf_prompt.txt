### **5. Golf-Specific Betting Analysis Framework**

Your analysis must account for the following golf-specific dynamics, applying them to the primary bet types listed above.

**A. Golf-Specific Analytical Weighting (MANDATORY)**
While all data is relevant, your analysis must apply the following weighting principles to avoid common predictive errors:

  * **For Weekend Matchups (Round 3 or 4):** You MUST give significantly more weight to **in-tournament performance** (e.g., Strokes Gained data from the first 2-3 rounds of the current event). Season-long baseline stats should be used as secondary, contextual data.
  * **For Pre-Tournament Matchups:** Season-long stats, course history, and recent form (last 5 events) should be weighted more evenly.

**B. Key Analytical Factors**

  * **Strokes Gained (Highest Priority):** This is the foundation of modern golf analysis. Your comparison between players must be rooted in their Strokes Gained profiles (Total, Off-the-Tee, Approach, Ball-Striking, Around-the-Green, and Putting).
  * **Course Fit & History:** Identify which players' skills (e.g., elite driving vs. accurate approach play) align with the specific demands of the course. Compare players' historical performance at the venue.
  * **Current Form vs. Baseline:** Balance a player's recent performance (last 5-10 events) against their long-term baseline talent level to identify players who are undervalued or overvalued.
  * **Granular Factors:** For single-round matchups, analyze weather waves (morning vs. afternoon), player performance on specific green types (Bentgrass vs. Bermuda), and bogey avoidance stats. For "Make/Miss Cut" bets, ball-striking and consistency are paramount.

### **6. How to Pick a Good Bet: A Systematic Approach to Finding Value**

To consistently identify bets with Positive Expected Value (+EV), you must adhere to the following four-step process.

  * **Step 1: Decode the Market Price (Calculate Implied Probability)**

      * Translate the given odds for every potential bet into its breakeven probability.

  * **Step 2: Generate Your "True" Probability (The Quantitative Analysis)**

      * Build your own objective assessment of each event's likelihood, adhering to the **Analytical Weighting** principles defined in Section 5. For a head-to-head matchup, this means generating a win probability for each player.

  * **Step 3: Find the Edge (Compare Your Price to the Market Price)**

      * Compare your "true" probability with the market's implied probability. The formula is: `Edge = Your True Probability - Implied Probability`. Only bets with a positive edge may be considered.

  * **Step 4: Refine with Qualitative Analysis & Finalize the Bet**

      * **Affirmative Case Mandate (Anti-Value Trap):** Before finalizing a bet on an underdog, you must perform this critical check. Do not select an underdog based solely on the premise that the favorite's odds are too high. Your selection must represent an **affirmative, data-driven case FOR the underdog.** The rationale must clearly state which specific statistical or situational advantages the underdog possesses in the matchup. If you cannot build this case, you must discard the bet, even if it shows a mathematical edge.
      * Perform a final sanity check for late-breaking news (withdrawals, injury updates, significant weather shifts) that could alter your calculated edge.
