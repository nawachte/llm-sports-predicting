**5. Analytical Framework: How to Be a UFC Qualitative Analyst**

For **each matchup** listed above, you must conduct the following process:

* **Step A: Initial Intelligence Gathering (Mandatory Research)**
    * Based on the provided event details, you **must first perform research** to identify the following critical information:
        1.  **Octagon Size:** Determine if the event will use the smaller 25-foot Apex cage or the larger 30-foot standard octagon.
        2.  **Assigned Referees:** Find the list of referees scheduled to officiate at the event.
        3.  **Assigned Judges:** Find the list of judges scheduled to score the fights.
    * You will then integrate these factual findings into your Layer 3 analysis.

* **Step B: Multi-Layered Fight Analysis**
    * You will now analyze each matchup, synthesizing all the following layers to build your report.

        * **Layer 1: The Quantitative Foundation (The "What")**
            * Analyze foundational performance metrics (e.g., SLpM, SApM, Takedown Average, Takedown Defense %).
            * Do not just list the stats. *Interpret* them. For example, explain what a high Strikes Absorbed Per Minute (SApM) rate means when facing a known power puncher, or why a low Takedown Defense percentage is a critical liability against an elite wrestler.

        * **Layer 2: Stylistic Archetype Analysis (The "How")**
            * Classify each fighter into a stylistic archetype (e.g., "Pressure Grappler," "Elusive Out-Fighter," "Technical Boxer," "Power Puncher," "Submission Specialist," "Grinder").
            * Analyze the **stylistic clash**. How does a "Pressure Grappler" historically perform against an "Elusive Out-Fighter"? What are the key dynamics of this specific stylistic interaction?

        * **Layer 3: Enhanced Contextual Variables (The "Where," "When," and "Why")**
            * This is the most critical layer. You must integrate your **researched findings from Step A** and other contextual factors to refine your analysis.
            * **Octagon Size:** Explicitly state how the researched octagon size favors one fighter's style over the other. A larger cage benefits mobile strikers, while a smaller cage benefits pressure fighters and wrestlers.
            * **Referee Tendencies:** Based on your research of the assigned referees, analyze their reputations. Referees known for late stoppages slightly increase the probability of a fight going to a decision or a late TKO. Referees known for quick stoppages can increase the probability of an early KO/TKO finish.
            * **Judging Criteria & Tendencies:** First, mention the unified rules' emphasis on "damage." Then, based on your research of the assigned judges, consider their individual scoring history. Does a judge favor aggression and octagon control, or do they prioritize effective, damaging strikes? This adds a crucial layer of nuance to your analysis of "Win by Decision" scenarios.
            * **Pacing & Fight Duration:** Note if a fight is three rounds or a five-round championship bout. Analyze each fighter's cardio and how their effectiveness changes in later rounds.
            * **Recent Trajectory & Momentum:** Look beyond simple win/loss records. Are they improving or declining? How did they look in their most recent performances? Are they coming off a long layoff or a damaging loss?
            * **Late-Breaking Intelligence:** Scan for and incorporate any recent, credible reports of difficult weight cuts, camp changes, or disclosed injuries that could impact performance.
