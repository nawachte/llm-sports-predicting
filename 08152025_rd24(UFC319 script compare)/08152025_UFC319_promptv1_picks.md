## **II. Portfolio Construction Methodology**

### **2.1. The Four-Step Value Identification Protocol**

A systematic, four-step protocol is applied to every potential wager to ensure a disciplined and objective selection process. This protocol is designed to move from market pricing to proprietary analysis, identifying and quantifying value.

* Step 1: Decode the Market Price (Calculate Implied Probability)  
  The initial step involves converting the provided American odds into their implied probability. This percentage represents the breakeven rate required for a bet to be profitable over the long term. It is the market's price for a given outcome, and it serves as the baseline against which all subsequent analysis is measured. The formulas used are:  
  * For Negative Odds (Favorites):  
    Implied Probability=(∣Odds∣+100)∣Odds∣​  
    \* For Positive Odds (Underdogs):  
    Implied Probability=(Odds+100)100​  
* Step 2: Generate "True" Probability (Proprietary Quantitative Analysis)  
  Independent of market odds, a proprietary probability is generated for each outcome. This model synthesizes a wide array of inputs, including foundational performance metrics (Significant Strikes Landed per Minute (SLpM), Significant Strikes Absorbed per Minute (SApM), Takedown Average, Takedown Defense) 3, situational factors (five-round experience, stylistic matchups), and qualitative overlays derived from expert fight analysis.7 The result is a unique, data-driven assessment of an event's likelihood.  
* Step 3: Find the Edge (Compare "True" Probability to Market Price)  
  The core of value identification occurs here. The proprietary "True" probability from Step 2 is compared directly against the market's implied probability from Step 1\. The difference between these two figures reveals the "edge." The formula is:

  Edge=True Probability−Implied Probability

  Only wagers demonstrating a clear positive edge are considered for inclusion in the portfolio. Wagers with a negative edge are immediately discarded as they represent negative expected value investments.  
* Step 4: Refine with Qualitative Analysis & Finalize  
  Before a bet is confirmed, a final qualitative review is conducted. This step serves as a sanity check for factors that may not be fully captured by the quantitative model. These include non-quantifiable elements such as fighter durability, fight IQ, camp stability, and potential motivational factors. If no significant red flags are raised during this final review, the wager is confirmed for portfolio inclusion.

### **2.2. Bankroll Allocation & Risk Management (The Kelly Criterion Framework)**

Bet sizing is a critical component of long-term portfolio management. Allocations are determined using a modified Kelly Criterion framework, which dictates that the size of a wager should be directly proportional to the size of its perceived edge. This mathematical approach ensures that capital is deployed most efficiently, with larger allocations directed toward opportunities with the highest and most reliable positive expected value. This strategy is designed to maximize long-term growth while mitigating the risk of ruin by avoiding oversized bets on low-edge propositions.

### **3.1. Gerald Meerschaert vs. Michal Oleksiejczuk**

* **Market Narrative:** The market prices this as a quintessential striker-versus-grappler matchup with a high probability of a finish. The odds for "Fight to Go the Distance: No" are a prohibitive \-380, and Michal Oleksiejczuk to win by KO/TKO/DQ is the favored outcome at \-115. This pricing reflects a strong belief in Oleksiejczuk's potent striking and a view that the fight will be decided by whichever fighter can impose their primary strength first.  
* Quantitative Deep Dive & Identification of Market Inefficiency:  
  This fight presents a clear and direct clash of a potent offensive weapon against a glaring defensive liability. Gerald "GM3" Meerschaert is one of the most prolific submission specialists in the history of the UFC's middleweight division, holding the record for most finishes in the division's history with 12.9 An astounding 29 of his 37 professional victories have come by way of submission.9

  Conversely, Michal Oleksiejczuk has demonstrated a consistent and critical vulnerability to grappling. Six of his nine professional losses have been by submission, accounting for 67% of his defeats.10 His UFC statistics reinforce this weakness, showing a takedown defense rate of only 48%.3 This statistical alignment—Meerschaert's elite submission offense against Oleksiejczuk's poor submission defense—is the central analytical pillar of this matchup.

  The market's pricing of "Gerald Meerschaert To Win By Submission" at \+400 carries an implied probability of just 20.0%. This valuation fails to adequately weigh the severity of Oleksiejczuk's defensive flaw against the proficiency of Meerschaert's primary win condition. Oleksiejczuk does not merely lose to grapplers; he is consistently finished by them. His UFC losses include submissions to Kevin Holland, Michel Pereira, Caio Borralho, Jimmy Crute, and Ovince Saint Preux.3 Meerschaert's entire game is built around creating the opportunities that these fighters capitalized on. The proprietary model calculates the true probability of this outcome to be approximately 35.0%, revealing a substantial \+15.0% edge and marking this as the premier value opportunity on the card.

### **3.2. Chase Hooper vs. Alexander Hernandez**

* **Market Narrative:** Chase Hooper is positioned as a significant favorite across the broader market, with moneyline odds reaching as high as \-345.8 This pricing is driven by his current five-fight win streak since moving up to the lightweight division and the general perception that his grappling is on a different level than that of Alexander Hernandez. The market correctly identifies Hooper's ground game as his primary path to victory.  
* Quantitative Deep Dive & Identification of Market Inefficiency:  
  Chase Hooper's statistical profile is that of a dedicated submission hunter. He has secured 8 of his 16 professional wins via submission and averages an aggressive 2.2 submission attempts per 15 minutes in the octagon.4 Since moving to lightweight, he has demonstrated a more effective pressure game, averaging 2.59 takedowns per 15 minutes to bring the fight to his world.4

  Alexander Hernandez possesses a respectable 71% takedown defense rate, but this statistic can be misleading.12 He has shown a tendency to fade under sustained pressure, and his defensive grappling discipline has been a liability, as seen in his submission loss to Renato Moicano.13 Expert analysis notes that while Hernandez no longer "completely implodes," he still "tires" when faced with extended adversity.8 This dynamic of attrition is crucial. Hernandez's best chance is an early knockout, whereas Hooper's probability of winning increases with each passing minute as he drains Hernandez's gas tank and exploits openings on the mat.

  The moneyline on Hooper is too wide to offer value, but the prop market presents a clear opportunity. The line for "Chase Hooper To Win By Submission" at \+150 has an implied probability of 40.0%. Given that 50% of his career wins have come by this method and he is facing an opponent whose primary weakness aligns perfectly with this strength, the true probability is modeled closer to 52.5%. This provides a \+12.5% edge on his most logical and statistically supported win condition.

### **3.3. Edson Barboza vs. Drakkar Klose**

* **Market Narrative:** This lightweight bout is priced as a competitive contest, with the veteran striker Edson Barboza holding a slight edge as a \-150 favorite in the wider market.14 The prevailing narrative pits Barboza's legendary kicking arsenal against the durable, grinding wrestling of Drakkar Klose. The market leans toward the fight going to a decision, with the "Fight to Go the Distance: Yes" line set at \-135.  
* Quantitative Deep Dive & Identification of Market Inefficiency:  
  This matchup is a study in attrition versus explosiveness. At 39 years old, Edson Barboza remains a dangerous striker with 14 career knockouts.15 However, his success is predicated on maintaining distance to unleash his kicks. Drakkar Klose's style is designed to negate this exact advantage. Klose is a pressure fighter who willingly absorbs strikes (3.54 SApM) to land his own (4.33 SLpM) and close the distance to initiate grappling exchanges.5 His path to victory involves smothering Barboza against the fence, forcing a high-paced clinch battle, and draining the cardio of the aging veteran.

  Klose's record demonstrates a clear pattern: he is a grinder, not a finisher. Nine of his 15 professional victories have come by decision.16 Barboza, despite his age, is exceptionally durable and has gone to the scorecards in 15 of his 36 professional fights.15 The logical conclusion of this stylistic clash is a grueling, three-round affair where Klose's pressure, control, and higher volume ultimately earn him the nod from the judges.

  The market has priced "Drakkar Klose To Win By Decision" at \+250, an implied probability of 28.6%. This significantly undervalues his most likely path to victory. Considering Klose's fighting style, Barboza's durability, and the age factor, the model places the true probability of a Klose decision victory at approximately 38.0%. This identifies a \+9.4% edge on a specific, high-value underdog outcome.

Despite his often-unorthodox appearance, he maintains a formidable pace and possesses knockout power late into fights, as evidenced by his fourth-round finish of Israel Adesanya.6 He lands an impressive 6.12 SLpM and has a proven gas tank.6 If the champion can weather the inevitable early storm from Chimaev in rounds one and two, the fight's entire dynamic is projected to invert. Chimaev's offensive output will likely decrease, while Du Plessis's pressure and power will become increasingly effective against a fatigued opponent.

  The market is overvaluing Chimaev's three-round dominance and undervaluing the five-round cardio variable. This creates a significant value opportunity on the champion. The line for "Dricus Du Plessis To Win By KO/TKO/DQ" at \+550 (15.4% implied probability) is particularly attractive.
